📖 QUICK REMINDER
Active Voice: The subject does the action. → "The coach locked the door."
Passive Voice: Something is done to the subject. → "The door was locked."

STATEMENT 1
❌ "The golden whistle took the thief!"
✏️ Write the correct Active Voice version:
Who actually took something? The whistle can't take anyone — only a person can do that! Think: [Who?] took [what?]
STATEMENT 2
❌ "I was heard a loud noise."
✏️ Write the correct Active Voice version:
"Was heard" is passive — but Mr. Jones is the one listening! He's the subject doing the hearing. Think: I [verb] a loud noise. No "was" needed!

🔬 FORENSICS LAB — PASSIVE VOICE ANALYSIS

The Lab Report

The lab team left notes in Active Voice. Rewrite them in Passive Voice for the official report.

👩‍🔬
DR. SYNTAX — FORENSICS
"Detective, my team found three pieces of evidence. I need you to rewrite my notes in Passive Voice for the official court report. Remember: in passive voice, the object becomes the subject, and we use was/were + past participle."
📖 PASSIVE VOICE FORMULA
Active: Someone dropped the flashlight.
Passive: The flashlight was dropped (on the floor).

Pattern: [Object] + was/were + [past participle]
📋 EVIDENCE NOTE 1
Active: "Someone dropped the flashlight on the floor."
✏️ Passive Voice version:
The flashlight is the object — it becomes the subject. Use was dropped. You can add "on the floor" at the end.
📋 EVIDENCE NOTE 2
Active: "Someone moved the referee's bench."
✏️ Passive Voice version:
"The referee's bench" becomes the subject. Use was moved.
📋 EVIDENCE NOTE 3
Active: "Someone left fingerprints on the broken window."
✏️ Passive Voice version:
"Fingerprints" is plural — use were left. Add "on the broken window" at the end.
