Instructional Design Case Study

Grammar Detective:
The Missing Golden Whistle

Gamified microlearning experience designed to teach Active & Passive Voice while reducing cognitive load for AuDHD and ESL learners.

▶ Play the Prototype
01 — The Problem

Grammar rules in isolation

For young ESL learners, especially those with ADHD or autism (AuDHD), traditional grammar drills—like transforming sentences from Active to Passive voice—create massive cognitive overload. They are presented with abstract rules and forced to perform repetitive tasks devoid of context or meaning.

"When grammar is taught without a clear 'why', engagement drops instantly. The solution isn't more repetition, but better context."
02 — The Solution

Narrative-driven microlearning

I designed "The Missing Golden Whistle" as a branching narrative where grammar is a tool to solve a mystery, not the end goal itself.

By breaking down complex concepts (like mixing Past Continuous and Past Simple) into small, interactive "match-up" tasks and drag-and-drop mechanics, we eliminate the need for heavy text walls. The technology handles the feedback instantly and without judgment (case-insensitive validation), freeing the instructor to focus on facilitating the logic and deduction.
